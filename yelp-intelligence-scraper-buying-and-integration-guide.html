Yelp Intelligence Scraper: buying and integration guide

24 Nov 2026 · benthepythondev

Short answer: The Yelp Intelligence Scraper exports public Yelp business data and review text. You can search by query and location (for example pizza in San Francisco, CA) or give it specific yelp.com/biz/ URLs. Each dataset row is one business, and that business's reviews are nested inside the row. It fits small business researchers, agencies comparing local competitors, and teams that track customer feedback. You don't need a Yelp account or an API key.

What you get

For each business:

  • business_id, business_name, url
  • rating (Yelp star rating) and review_count (Yelp's total review count for the business)
  • category, address, phone, price_range
  • website and hours (often empty, see limits below)
  • reviews_count (how many reviews this run actually extracted), detail_enriched, scraped_at
  • an optional lead_score, calculated from rating, review count and the sentiment of the sampled reviews

For each review inside a business row:

  • review_id, rating, full text, date, reviewer_name, reviewer_location
  • an optional sentiment label (positive, neutral and so on) from transparent rules

If Yelp doesn't show a value, the field stays null.

Commercial use cases

  1. Local competitor comparison. Pull the ratings, review counts, categories and price ranges for every business that shows up for one query in one area, then compare them side by side.
  2. Customer feedback tracking. Collect full review text with dates and star ratings for your own business or a competitor's. Scheduled runs let you watch the feedback change over time.
  3. Business research lists. Build a list of local businesses with names, addresses and phone numbers, then sort it by lead_score or rating.
  4. Review text analysis. Export the nested reviews as JSON and feed them into your own tagging or reporting.

When to use this vs manual export or your own scraper

Yelp has no built-in export for this data, so the manual option is copying it out by hand. That works for a business or two. Once you need many queries, many locations or repeat runs, it stops being practical.

Writing your own scraper means solving the access problem yourself. The README says Yelp refuses later search pages and business detail pages to visitors. This Actor runs through Apify Unblocker by default, and if a source is blocked the run fails visibly instead of returning an empty "successful" report. You also get deduplication of business and review IDs within a run, rule-based sentiment and lead scores, and per-business pricing.

If you need website and hours for every business, or more than about 10 businesses from a single search, read the limits below first.

FAQ

I'm new to Apify. Where do I find the results?

When the run finishes, open the dataset. The Overview view shows one row per business. The Reviews view shows one row per review. Export JSON if you want the complete review text. Export CSV or Excel if you want a flat business table for a spreadsheet.

Why did I get fewer businesses than I set in maxBusinesses?

Yelp only shows visitors the first page of a search, which has up to 10 organic businesses (ads are excluded). The README's test searches returned between 3 and 10 per search. To get more, run more queries (for example dentist and orthodontist) or more locations (neighbourhoods, postal codes). Rating filters can also lower the count, because they don't trigger a search for replacements.

What is the difference between review_count and reviews_count?

review_count is Yelp's total for the business. reviews_count is how many reviews this run extracted.

Are reviews newest first?

No. They come in Yelp's recommended order.

Honest limits

  • Each search query and location returns at most about 10 businesses.
  • In search mode, website and hours are usually null (detail_enriched: false), because Yelp refuses business pages to visitors. In the sample run, one business also had an empty phone.
  • Sentiment comes from rules, not a deep language model. In the sample, a 5 star review that says the pizza was "SO worth the wait" is labelled neutral. Treat the label as a rough signal.
  • You can request up to 100 reviews per business, but only up to 25 are covered by the per-business price.
  • Counts can change while Yelp updates its pages. Scheduled runs can return the same business again on purpose.
