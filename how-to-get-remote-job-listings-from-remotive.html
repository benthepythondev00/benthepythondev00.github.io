How to get remote job listings from Remotive with salary ranges and a quality score

19 Nov 2026 · benthepythondev

Remotive is a job board for fully remote roles. If you want those listings as rows you can filter, sort or load into a spreadsheet, the Remotive Remote Jobs Intelligence Actor on Apify reads Remotive's live jobs feed and gives you one record per job. It also splits the salary text into separate numbers and adds a 0 to 100 opportunity score. You don't need an API key or a login.

If you want Remotive and five other boards in one dataset, see the six job boards guide instead.

What you get

Each job comes back as one row with these fields:

  • id: Remotive's job id.
  • title and company_name, plus company_logo (a URL to the logo).
  • category: for example Software Development or Marketing.
  • tags: the skills and topics attached to the job, such as react, postgresql, shopify or video editing.
  • job_type: one of full_time, contract, freelance, part_time, internship.
  • publication_date: when the job was posted.
  • location: who can apply, for example "USA, Canada" or "Worldwide".
  • salary: the salary text exactly as the employer wrote it.
  • salary_min, salary_max, salary_currency: the same salary as numbers.
  • url: the Remotive page for the job, where people apply.
  • description_short: the first part of the job description. You can also ask for the full text.
  • opportunity_score: a score from 0 to 100. The README says it combines four factors, two of which are the job category (high-demand tech roles score higher) and the salary level (a higher maximum salary scores higher).

Filters you can set

Every input is optional. If you set none of them, you get all current jobs.

  • categories: Software Development, DevOps / Sysadmin, Data Science, Design, Product, Customer Support, Marketing, Sales, Writing, Finance / Legal, Human Resources, All others.
  • jobTypes: full_time, contract, freelance, part_time, internship.
  • keywords: keeps only jobs that contain the word in the title, description, company or tags.
  • locations: for example USA, Europe, Worldwide.
  • minSalary: the lowest salary you want, in USD (0 means no filter).
  • maxResults: how many jobs to return, from 1 to 2000.
  • includeOpportunityScore and includeFullDescription: turn the score on, and choose whether to get the full description.

This example input asks for Python or AWS jobs in software development or DevOps, open to the USA, paying at least $120k:

{
  "categories": ["Software Development", "DevOps / Sysadmin"],
  "jobTypes": ["full_time", "contract"],
  "keywords": ["python", "aws"],
  "locations": ["USA"],
  "minSalary": 120000,
  "maxResults": 100,
  "includeOpportunityScore": true,
  "includeFullDescription": false
}

Use cases

These come from the Actor's README:

  1. Remote job boards and aggregators. Fill a niche board, such as one for developers, designers or data roles, with fresh filtered listings ranked by the score.
  2. Recruitment and talent sourcing. See which companies are hiring remotely, compare salary ranges and find well-paid roles early.
  3. Remote work market research. Look at demand by category, salary trends and location requirements over time, for example by running it on a schedule and keeping each run.
  4. Job alerts and career tools. Send email digests or Slack or Discord bot messages with scored job picks.

Honest limits

  • Remotive only. The data covers only jobs posted on Remotive, not the whole remote job market.
  • The salary parser can get odd salary text wrong. In the same real run, "Remote Office Assistant" at Coalition Technologies had the salary text "$35,3k- $52k". It came out as salary_min: 52000 and salary_max: 353000, which is clearly not what the employer meant. Check the original salary text before you use the numbers, especially when the max is far above the min.
  • Not every job lists a salary. Jobs without one can't be ranked on pay. Keep that in mind when you set minSalary.
  • Tags come from the listing and can be loose. The office assistant role above was tagged with things like php, magento and bootstrap. Tags are a rough signal, not a verified skill list.
  • The score is a ranking aid. It is based on factors like category and salary. It says nothing about the employer, the team or the hiring process, and a bad salary parse can push a score up.
  • Short descriptions are cut off. Turn on includeFullDescription if you need the full text.
  • Location is a free-text field. Values like "USA, Canada, USA timezones" mix countries and time zones, so location filters match text, not exact regions.
