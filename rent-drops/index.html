Apartment concessions and net-effective rents in 50 US metros

Snapshot November 26, 2026 · 4,862 Apartments.com properties and 4,992 Zumper listings · publicly advertised asking rents · updated weekly

The November 26, 2026 snapshot covers 50 US metros, 4,862 Apartments.com properties and 4,992 Zumper listings with an advertised price. Across all metros, 2,464 of 4,862 Apartments.com properties (51%) advertised a concrete move-in concession such as weeks free, a dollar amount off or look-and-lease; generic taglines were not counted. The median metro sat at 55%. The highest shares were in Austin, TX (77%), Phoenix, AZ (74%), Denver, CO (70%); the lowest was New York, NY at 10%. Each metro page shows Studio to 3BR headline and net-effective asking rents (published only from 20 or more floor plans), the Zumper-to-Apartments.com price gap, a 25-row CSV sample with no login, and a calculator for any special. Figures are publicly advertised asking rents and specials, not signed leases or private rent rolls.

Method

Publicly advertised asking rents and move-in specials. Not signed lease transactions, not private rent-roll data, not pricing or legal advice.

Net-effective rent
asking rent x (1 - free months / 12) for weeks or months free; asking rent - dollar-off / 12 for dollar-off and look & lease; asking rent x (1 - pct x months / 12) for percent-off. Waived fees do not change rent.
Concession value
Largest concrete value in the advertised special ('up to', 'select units'), so the net-effective figure is the maximum advertised saving, not a guaranteed one.
Already-net rates
Specials saying the advertised rate already reflects the concession are counted as concessions but never discounted twice.
Concession share
Share of priced Apartments.com properties in the metro sample whose special matches the strict concession regex. Zumper has no specials field.
Bedroom medians
Published only from 20 or more floor plans. Floor plans with 2+ bedrooms advertised below 75% of the metro 1BR median, below the same property's cheapest 1BR, or named as student / co-living are excluded.
Rent cuts
A rent cut is the same listing id advertising a lower asking rent than its highest rent in the last 30 days. Shown only once 4 distinct ISO weeks of snapshots exist; window_label names the real span (4 weekly snapshots = a 3-week change, '30-day' from 28 days on).

Questions

Which US cities have the most apartment concessions right now?

Sorted by the share of priced Apartments.com properties advertising a concrete special in the November 26, 2026 snapshot: Austin, TX 77%, Phoenix, AZ 74%, Denver, CO 70%, Raleigh, NC 70%, Charlotte, NC 70%.

What counts as a concession here?

Only specials with a value we can parse: weeks or months free, a percent off for a set number of months, a dollar amount off, look-and-lease deals and waived fees. Taglines such as 'ask about our specials', gift cards and 'rents starting at' prices are not counted.

How is net-effective rent calculated?

Asking rent times (1 minus free months divided by lease months). Weeks free convert to months at 52/12 weeks a month; a dollar amount off is divided by the lease length. Waived fees are one-time and do not change the monthly rent.

Why are some bedroom medians missing?

A median is shown only when at least 20 floor plans of that size exist in the metro sample, after removing student, co-living and per-bed plans. Below that, the page shows the count and no number.

When do the 30-day rent cuts appear?

After four weekly snapshots of the same listing ids. Until then no rent-cut count is published.

Are these signed lease rents?

No. They are publicly advertised asking rents and move-in specials, not signed leases, not private rent-roll data and not pricing or legal advice.
