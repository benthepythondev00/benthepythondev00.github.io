Why the card lock matters to a list buyer

Most contact-sheet complaints come down to two failure modes: the same business appearing three times under slightly different listings, and emails glued onto the wrong company. This card locks against both. Deduplication runs on place_id — Google's stable identifier for a physical place — so a chain location and its duplicate map entries collapse to one row each. And the leftover-email counter makes misassignment visible instead of silent: if the crawl finds an address it cannot attribute, that address is reported as leftover, not padded into a row. For an agency building a Berlin outreach list, a franchise team scouting a territory, or a researcher sizing a niche, that means the row count you pay for is the business count you get.

How the extraction works

1. Stable identity

place_id is the primary identity. One contact row per unique place — duplicate map listings collapse instead of inflating the sheet.

2. Website-sourced email

The crawl visits the business's own site and records where each email was found in email_source. DACH Impressum pages are supported.

3. Confidence, not guesses

email_confidence scores the evidence behind each address. The email-verify option stayed off in this run; no mailbox checks are implied.

4. Leftover-email honesty

An email that cannot be tied to a specific place is counted as leftover rather than forced onto a row. This run finished at 0.
