What does RoofSquare cost, and are there any per-job fees?

One flat price, $0 per-job fees, and $0 complex-roof surcharges. The roof calculator is free forever, and your first 2 approved homeowner deposits are free. After 2 approved deposits, RoofSquare Pro costs $39.99/month or $299.99/year (which includes a 7-day free trial). RoofSquare always shows the full renewal price and exact first charge date on screen before any trial starts.

Is the roof calculator really free forever?

Yes. The roof calculator is never paywalled, even after you use your 2 free approved deposits or cancel Pro. You can enter unlimited roof planes with feet, inches, 1/8-inch fractions, pitch factor, and waste percentage completely offline on a roof and get squares plus a full materials list at no cost.

How do I cancel RoofSquare Pro?

Open iOS Settings > Apple Account > Subscriptions > RoofSquare > Cancel Subscription, or open Apple Subscriptions directly from RoofSquare Settings. There are no phone calls, no retention forms, and no credit pulls. After cancelling, every quote and PDF you already created stays readable and exportable offline forever.

How do homeowner deposits and California's deposit cap work?

Paste your own Stripe, Square, or PayPal HTTPS payment link in RoofSquare Settings, review the sample deposit and state cap, tap 1. Open Payment Link, and tap 2. Mark Link Working before sending your first quote (sending is blocked until your deposit link is checked). Homeowner deposits go directly to your own payment account — never through Apple In-App Purchase, and RoofSquare takes 0% of your job deposits. For California jobs, RoofSquare enforces the statutory cap under California Bus. & Prof. Code § 7159.5 ($1,000 or 10% of the contract price, whichever is less), prints the required wording, and links to the CSLB guide. RoofSquare is not legal advice; verify your state and local contractor rules before collecting a deposit.

How do I calculate roofing squares with pitch and waste?

Multiply each roof plane's plan length by plan width (using feet, inches, and fractions), multiply by the pitch factor sqrt(1 + (rise / 12)²), sum all planes, add your waste percentage (typically 10% for gable roofs or 15% for hip roofs), and divide by 100 to get roofing squares. For example, two 40 ft × 20 ft planes at 6/12 pitch (factor 1.1180) equal 1,788.85 slope sq ft; with 10% waste that is 1,967.74 sq ft, or 19.68 squares (order 20 squares / 60 shingle bundles).
