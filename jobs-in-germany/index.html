How the who-can-apply check works

The tag on each job comes from the employer's own words, not from a guess. We read the full German ad and look for a sentence about applicants from abroad, EU citizenship or work permits. When we find one, we quote it under the summary with an English translation, so you can judge it yourself.

Applicants from abroad welcome
The ad invites applicants or qualifications from abroad, or mentions recognition of a foreign degree, an adaptation course, visa help or relocation. Company boilerplate like "we work worldwide" does not count.
EU/EEA residents only
The ad asks for EU citizenship or for applicants who already live in the EU. These jobs are hidden when you choose "Outside the EU".
Asks for an existing work permit
The ad asks for a work or residence permit you already hold. It stays visible for EU residents, who don't need one.
Ad doesn't say who can apply
Most ads say nothing either way. That isn't a no: many of these employers hire from abroad when the qualification fits.

The Blue Card check

An EU Blue Card needs a job offer paying at least €50,700 gross a year in 2026, or €45,934.20 in shortage occupations such as IT, engineering, medicine and nursing with a degree (Federal Foreign Office). We compare the lowest salary in the ad's own salary field with that line. Full-time hourly pay counts at 40 hours a week. A Blue Card also needs a university degree or, in IT, three years of experience, so most trade jobs go through the skilled-worker visa instead, which has no fixed salary line under age 45.

The Opportunity Card tag

On an Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte) you can look for work in Germany for up to a year and work up to 20 hours a week while you search, plus trial work of up to two weeks (§ 20a AufenthG). We tag ads that offer part-time hours and don't exclude applicants from outside the EU.

What the summary is

The English title is a machine translation. The summary line is built from the ad's own data fields (hours, contract, salary, German level), so it can't misquote the ad. Full-time hourly pay is shown per year at 40 hours a week; part-time pay stays per hour. The full German ad, and the way to apply, are on arbeitsagentur.de behind each "Original listing" link.

Why German ads matter

Indeed's research found that only 2.7% of job ads in Germany say German isn't required, and most of those are low-wage jobs. Skilled jobs that ask for some German are where the real demand is, and many employers pay for language courses.
